# Local livelihoods: twelve additional specialist branches

This batch adds **12 branches and 48 stage purchases**. The catalogue now has **123 specialist branches / 492 stages**, plus the existing **16 rotations / 64 stages**. Each branch still needs the matching adjacent settlement/city tier and its earlier stages. The recently reduced specialist and tier III–IV prices apply unchanged, with craft-specific materials, coal paid at construction, and no upkeep.

The figures below describe the total benefit at stages I / II / III / IV, not a fresh additional amount from every purchased stage. Harvest budgets are per producer and shared across the listed seasons; collection still requires the matching dice roll. Town and harvesting-unit rules keep their existing multipliers. Floods, ice, occupation and ravaged infrastructure still block production under the usual rules.

| Branch | Location | Distinct benefit |
|---|---|---|
| **Rice–fish refuges** | Rice or flood-rice in Tropical, Subtropical, Monsoon or Mesoamerican climates, with freshwater access | 1/2/3/4 Fish shared across productive rice seasons **during wet spells**. Cultured fish do not depend on migrating shoals. Flooded fields remain closed. |
| **Spring sap groves** | Woods, river woods or old-growth forest in Temperate, Cold or Oceanic climates | 1/2/3/4 Grain **in spring**, representing syrup from suitable maple/birch stands. Dry spells or prolonged cold halve the increment, rounded down. |
| **Edible seaweed lines** | Coastal/shallow water in Cold, Temperate, Oceanic or Temperate Rainforest climates | 1/2/3/4 Fish shared between **spring and autumn**, representing edible seafood. Independent of shoals; frozen water still closes collection. Marine harvesters recognize these cultivated sites. |
| **Winter reed beds** | Rivers/lakes in Temperate, Oceanic or Mediterranean climates | 1/2/3/4 Wood **in winter**, representing mature thatch and light building material. Water must remain accessible rather than frozen. |
| **Coconut fibre yards** | Tropical Maritime island palms | Future works on the same tile save **10/20/30/40% of Wool and Cloth**, rounded down and capped at 1/2/3/4 of each per purchase. Coir substitutes for cordage/padding requirements. |
| **Mine stone stowing** | Mineral mines, excluding peat | Future works on the same tile save **10/20/30/40% of Stone and Blocks**, rounded down and capped at 1/2/3/4 of each per purchase. Sorted waste rock replaces part of local foundations and fill. |
| **Date-pit feed mills** | Desert, Hyperarid or Semiarid oases | 1/2/3/4 Meat shared across seasons **immediately after the largest date harvests**. Stored, milled residues supply sheltered livestock feed. The date harvest is retained. |
| **Upland leaf-hay lofts** | Broadleaved woods/river woods above elevation 0.65 in Temperate, Oceanic or Alpine climates | 1/2/3/4 Meat shared across the **leanest timber seasons**. Conserved leafy branches feed domestic livestock; hunters do not collect this output. |
| **Brine graduation walls** | Salt-producing sites in Temperate, Oceanic or Cold climates | Each stage reduces the **remaining wet-weather salt loss by 10%**, following the existing protection cap and whole-card rounding. Brushwood concentration works help damp-climate salt production. |
| **Fog-catching orchard screens** | **Coastal** Desert or Semiarid oases | Each stage reduces the **remaining dry-weather grain loss by 10%**. This models modest supplementary fog water; it does not grant freshwater access to unrelated projects or create a new harvest season. |
| **Recession fish pools** | Flood-wheat/flood-sorghum in Desert, Semiarid, Savanna, Monsoon or Subtropical climates, with freshwater access | During wet spells, 1/2/3/4 Fish shared across **idle seasons after a cereal harvest**. Standing crops or active secondary rotations take priority. This represents retained side pools; actual flood closure remains. |
| **Musk-ox underwool gathering** | Arctic, Glacial or Tundra snow plains, musk-ox range or tundra heath | 1/2/3/4 extra Wool **in spring while musk oxen are present**. Hunters can collect it. Reindeer do not qualify; the bonus ends when the musk oxen leave. |

## Limits and balance

- Equivalent services use the strongest local capacity. Existing production, rotations and infrastructure remain intact.
- Construction substitutions apply only to later works on that same tile and only for their owner. They do not reduce coal, award stock directly, or discount any material-reuse workshop. Overlapping Cloth savings use the best eligible workshop rather than stacking percentages.
- Four seasonal bonus cards at stage IV is an annual profile budget, not four bonus cards in each season. Weather-sensitive and idle-season branches may deliver less or nothing when their conditions are absent.
- Protection can round to no extra card on a low-yield site. The interface compares actual rounded harvests and states the reduction in losses separately.
- These additions work on eligible tiles in existing saves. No new map, resource type, tile art or recurring fuel payment is required.

## Research and game abstractions

The techniques are historically or ecologically grounded; the exact card budgets, seasons and prices are balancing choices rather than measured agricultural forecasts. Grain represents gathered sweet foods as well as crops; Fish represents edible coastal seafood including seaweed. Fog is approximated by dry coastal geography because the game has no independent fog-frequency simulation. Mine stone reuse represents suitable sorted rock, not universal reuse of every tailings material.
