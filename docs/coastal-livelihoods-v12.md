# Coastal variety and local livelihoods

## Seals

Seals require **open, wild, marine-coastal land in Arctic, Glacial or Tundra climates**. Ordinary Cold forests, inland snowfields, lakeshores, crop fields and mines do not qualify. Their coastal-only rule is intentional; the previous rarity came from combining these habitat restrictions with a 22% animal roll and competition from reindeer and musk ox.

Eligible shores now use a 50% animal roll, with seals weighted 7 against 3 each for musk ox and reindeer: about **27% seals per eligible shore**, compared with about 9% previously. If those rolls leave a map without any seals despite eligible habitat, one coastal population is selected deterministically. Existing herds are retained. Old campaigns receive a one-time survey of eligible shores when loaded; the saved survey flag prevents repeated population additions. New expeditions survey only their new eligible habitat. No annual regeneration or hunting extinction is introduced.

Seals can traverse up to three tiles, including short marine crossings within two tiles of land, and still finish on an eligible coastal land tile. Open rivers and lakes do not become seal migration corridors. Existing winter ice crossings remain available. New maps give gentle polar shores additional open snow-plain habitat, while steep mountain coasts keep their existing relief. Existing seasonal seal artwork is preserved.

## New coast patterns

Generation 12 retains the continental, broken-coast and island-sea world structures. Non-continental worlds now have four seeded patterns, visible in the terrain inspector:

- **Island mosaic:** compact islands with varied orientation.
- **Ribbon islands:** elongated islands and parallel narrow passages, making landing sites and cross-island routes more important.
- **Sweeping island arcs:** gradual curvature bends groups of islands and channels across the region.
- **Crossed straits:** oblique channels and staggered land strips create less regular approaches and crossing points.

These transform the continuous physical elevation field, before terrain, drainage and climate selection. They do not scatter isolated water tiles afterward. Existing lake limits, downhill rivers, pass spacing and climate compatibility still apply. Generation 11 and earlier saves retain their original geography, including expeditions. Start a new campaign for these coast patterns.

## Eight new specialist branches

Each branch has four separately purchased stages, requires the corresponding adjacent settlement/city tier, uses its own craft recipe and can be ravaged. Coal is paid up front from tier II; there is no upkeep. Total catalogue: **111 specialist branches / 444 purchases**, plus **16 rotations / 64 purchases**.

| Branch | Eligible site | Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Coppice rotations | Temperate, Oceanic or Mediterranean broadleaved woods | 1/2/3/4 Wood in winter from managed pole regrowth. |
| Riverbank pollards | Temperate or Oceanic broadleaved river woods with freshwater access | The same winter pole budget. Coppice and pollards use the strongest capacity, rather than stacking duplicate output. |
| Heath apiaries | Tundra heath | 1/2/3/4 Grain in summer, representing honey. Drought or cold halves the increment, rounded down. |
| Meadow apiaries | Suitable flower-rich pastures in Temperate, Oceanic, Prairie or Mediterranean climates | The same summer honey budget and weather exposure. |
| Rice–duck husbandry | Tropical, Subtropical, Monsoon or Mesoamerican rice fields with freshwater | During wet spells, 1/2/3/4 Meat shared across productive rice seasons. Flood closures still prevent harvest. |
| Woodland pannage | Temperate or Oceanic broadleaved woods | 1/2/3/4 Meat in autumn from domestic pigs feeding on mast; drought halves the increment. Independent of wild herds and unavailable to hunters. |
| Seal haul-out wardens | Eligible polar coastal hunting habitat | Improves hide recovery while reducing local development disturbance by 15/30/45/60%. Seals still migrate; other refuge works do not multiply the same protection. |
| Reusable stone cradles | Stone quarries | Later works on the same tile save 10/20/30/40% of Planks and Cloth, rounded down and capped at 1/2/3/4 each. Reuse-yard construction and coal receive no discount. |

All yields are per producer on a matching roll. Seasonal budgets are shared, not awarded in full in every season. Ownership, access, flooding, ice and occupation restrictions remain. Original branches, rotations, saved projects and artwork remain valid.

The numerical effects are game balance choices. Techniques are informed by [Forest Research on coppice and pollards](https://cdn.forestresearch.gov.uk/1984/04/fclf083_1984.pdf), [FAO on rice–duck husbandry](https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/1618289/), [FAO on honey and forage](https://www.fao.org/4/y5110e/y5110e05.htm), and [Forestry England on pannage](https://www.forestryengland.uk/new-forest/pannage-pigs-and-acorns).

## More approachable investment costs

Every specialist stage, including crop rotations, receives about **15% off** its finished construction bill. Main production infrastructure is about **15% cheaper at tier III** and **25% cheaper at tier IV**. Specialists use those cheaper late-tier base budgets and then receive their own reduction, so late specialist investments improve further. Material identity, technology/city requirements, output and protection are unchanged. Whole-card rounding keeps at least one of every required ingredient. Coal stays a substantial one-time construction material, with no upkeep.

For example, the standard irrigation coal bills are now **6 / 34 / 75** at tiers II / III / IV, previously 6 / 40 / 100. These reductions apply to future construction in existing campaigns as well as new games.
