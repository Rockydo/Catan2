# Crop yields and game balance

Research reviewed: 2026-09-20.

The game uses crop type, growing conditions and harvest frequency to give climates different food economies. Grain cards are an abstract food resource used by cereals, roots, gardens and Olive groves, not tonnes of a particular cereal. The chosen numbers below are balance decisions informed by agricultural research.

## What the research supports

A yield in tonnes per hectare per harvest is different from production per hectare per year. Annual production adds the crops harvested from the same physical field. Two crops do not necessarily produce less than three: a longer or better growing season can produce more grain per crop. IRRI describes higher per-crop rice yields in cooler regions, alongside the greater annual cropping opportunities available in the tropics. [IRRI, Rice in the Global Economy](https://books.irri.org/9789712202582_content.pdf)

Triple rice cropping also requires suitable varieties, water and farm management. IRRI's long-running experiment adopted continuous triple cropping when short-duration, high-yielding varieties became available. Tropical weather alone does not guarantee three harvests. [IRRI, Long-Term Continuous Cropping Experiment](https://lte.irri.org/ltcce)

Rice yields normally refer to paddy, which includes the husk. A modern multistage mill typically recovers 65-70% of paddy weight as milled rice, including broken grains. Comparing paddy tonnes directly with usable wheat grain overstates rice's food advantage. Flour extraction and other processing can also change the usable output of other cereals. The game does not simulate these processing losses. [IRRI, Milling yields](https://www.knowledgebank.irri.org/step-by-step-production/postharvest/milling/producing-good-quality-milled-rice/milling-yields)

These reference points describe particular conditions. They are not a controlled comparison of every crop, nor a ranking derived from national or global averages.

Barley is not inherently half as productive as wheat. FAO describes approximately 3 t/ha expected farm potential for winter wheat and at least 3 t/ha for spring barley in its DPRK assessment. Lower game yields represent fields with more limited growing conditions, rather than a universal biological disadvantage. [FAO crop assessment](https://www.fao.org/4/Y4756E/y4756e08.htm)

For UK conditions, the RHS places first-early potato harvests in June and July and maincrop harvests from late August to October, with maincrop varieties producing a larger harvest. A Potato field represents a mix of early and maincrop varieties across the terrain. The game uses a shared seasonal calendar rather than literal local months; this reference supports the early/maincrop distinction, not a date claim for Andean agriculture. Its Summer and Autumn windows do not imply two complete potato crops from the same plot in one year. [RHS, How to grow potatoes](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/potatoes/grow-your-own)

Turnips also have early Summer and Autumn maincrop windows. The RHS recommends cool conditions and distinguishes early sowings from March to June from maincrop sowings in July and August. These dates support the game’s two windows; the 1:3 allocation between them is a balance choice. [RHS, How to grow turnips](https://www.rhs.org.uk/vegetables/turnips/grow-your-own)

Oats are an Old World temperate crop: Kew identifies a western Asian cultivated origin, while Teagasc documents their suitability for Ireland. Teagasc places cereal harvests mainly in August within a July-to-September season. The game therefore assigns most Oat production to Summer and a smaller share to Autumn. “Traditionally grown in Europe” does not mean “botanically native to Europe.” Sorghum has an African origin and suits the warm Old World food role. [Kew, Avena sativa](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/391732-1), [Teagasc, Oats](https://teagasc.ie/news--events/daily/oats-a-crop-with-more-value-than-the-market-realises/), [Teagasc, Harvests](https://teagasc.ie/crops/crops/cereal-crops/spring-cereals/harvests/), [FAO, Sorghum and millets](https://www.fao.org/4/t0818e/t0818e01.htm)

Chinampas are fertile cultivated beds associated with canals, with diverse crops and intensive management. Their three game harvest windows model that managed food system; the annual baseline of 3 is a balance decision. [FAO, Chinampas agricultural heritage](https://www.fao.org/giahs/giahs-around-the-world/mexico-chinampas-agricultural-system/en)

## Chosen game balance

Each entry is Grain per settlement on a matching dice roll. Every matching roll in an active season pays; these are not automatic seasonal deliveries or a limit of one payout per harvest. The four seasonal amounts sum to four times the annual baseline. With comparable dice opportunities, that baseline is the expected average across the calendar.

The rice totals of 12, 8 and 4 make three, two and one harvest windows easy to understand. They deliberately simplify differences in per-crop productivity. Research does not prove that two subtropical crops must produce less than three tropical crops. Tropical rice has a strong annual role, subtropical rice matches ordinary wheat's annual baseline with two harvest windows, and rainfed Monsoon rice provides a smaller, concentrated harvest.

Maize and ordinary wheat share a broad production tier for balance, despite maize's higher potential in suitable conditions. Oceanic barley and Temperate/Oceanic turnips represent productive fields. Their poorer-climate counterparts and millet retain a smaller harvest. These are abstractions of growing conditions, not fixed yield ratios between species.

## Regional crop roles and migration

Turnip fields replace Rye at unchanged land-generation shares: 4% Temperate, 2% Oceanic, 3% Cold and 3% Alpine. Their annual baseline stays 2 in Temperate/Oceanic climates and 1 in Cold/Alpine climates. Summer pays one baseline and Autumn three. The smaller early harvest improves reliability while the Autumn maincrop remains the largest payout, without increasing annual expected output.

Oats replace the 4% Temperate Maize interval, with Summer/Autumn output 6/2. Sorghum replaces the 5% Subtropical Maize interval, with Autumn output 8. Both retain baseline 2. New Potato fields occur only in Andean regions (18% of land), while Maize occurs in Prairie (12%) and Mesoamerican regions (20%). Mesoamerican Chinampa gardens occupy 6% of land. These assignments distinguish regional economies; the game does not simulate global crop introductions.

Save version 12 converts retired `rye-fields` and prototype non-American `potato-fields` to `turnip-fields`. Non-American Maize becomes Oats in cool climates or Sorghum in warm climates. American Maize and Potato fields retain their identity. Climate, dice number, tile ID, camps, workshops, units, random streams and stored goods survive. Current seasonal schedules apply on load without retroactive harvests.

Prairie Sunflower fields add an oilseed crop without a new resource: baseline 1 Oil, paid as 4 Oil in Autumn. Oil retains its 1:1 Coal substitution and Fuel processing, including the linked workshop and automatic advanced production.

## Black-soil wheat

Chernozem is a soil type associated with steppe and forest-steppe, including Ukraine. FAO describes these soils as among the world's prime wheat soils, while also identifying rainfall and heat during grain filling as constraints. Fertile soil does not make every location equally productive. [FAO, Farming Systems and Poverty](https://www.fao.org/4/y1860e/y1860e06.htm)

Black-soil wheat is therefore a rare fertile-field biome within existing climates, rather than a separate climate. Its Summer payout of 12 provides a valuable but concentrated food source. The 50% bonus over ordinary wheat is a game decision, not a universal measured Chernozem multiplier.

- Temperate: 2% of land terrain, replacing two percentage points of Golden fields.
- Steppe: 4% of land terrain, replacing four percentage points of Steppe plain.

These percentages are conditional on generating land. No other terrain shares change for this addition.

## Shared implementation rule

Use the climate-aware `biomeYield` helper as the common source of annual baseline yields. Seasonal production must sum to four times that value for each resource. Actual production, AI valuation and exploration, setup resources, workshops and reference tables must use the same baseline.

Reducing rice's seasonal payouts without updating its baseline would leave other systems valuing or producing the old amount. Use the shared helper for climate differences. Turnip fields replace retired Rye through save migration; American Potato fields keep a separate terrain ID; Black-soil wheat is a separate named biome. The figures above revise annual production deliberately, while preserving the relationship between each revised baseline and its seasonal calendar.
